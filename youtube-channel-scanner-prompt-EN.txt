1. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────

This report is a first step toward understanding what the creator has already published and the visible results accumulated so far.

It should help the creator recognize:
- What they have published.
- Who the published content appears to be for.
- What topics and needs it addresses.
- How their posts relate to one another.
- Which groups accumulate their views.
- What share of views comes from Shorts and from Videos.
- What concrete test they could run next.

It does not research the market, the actual audience, or competitors.

It does not determine:
- Why the channel is not growing.
- What their entire audience wants.
- Whether a niche is validated.
- Whether sufficient external demand exists.
- Which topics have low competition.
- Whether the content delivers value.
- Whether the channel will achieve growth or monetization.

Do not promise results.

The document must be easier to understand than a YouTube Studio listing. Its audience is a human being, not an AI.

10. CONTENT GROUPS
────────────────────────────────────────

UNIVERSAL APPLICATION

This tool analyzes posts on any subject.
It does not use preset topic categories.

Derive groups exclusively from the received list.
Do not reuse topics, categories, or priorities from other channels,
examples, tests, or earlier reports.

Groups are not mandatory.
Do not automatically treat variety as a flaw.

In the report, use “groups” or “content groups.”
Do not automatically call them pillars, angles, niches,
or audience segments.

A. IDENTIFY EACH POST

Before grouping, internally identify:
- The specific subject.
- The activity, experience, or type of content presented.
- The main question, need, or decision, if any.
- The snippet from the title or description supporting that reading.
- Whether available information is insufficient or ambiguous.

Not every post solves a problem.
Posts can entertain, narrate, show a performance, teach
a process, express an idea, or share an experience.

Do not invent purposes not supported by the text.

Separate main content from:
- Secondary mentions.
- Tools used.
- Hashtags.
- Commercial calls to action.
- Repeated boilerplate in descriptions.

B. FORM THE GROUPS

Propose groups based on specific connections among
posts, not a fixed number of buckets to fill.

For each group, internally define:
- A short name.
- A shared core.
- Conditions that allow a post to be included.
- Conditions that exclude a post.

The shared core must explain a useful, concrete connection:
a bounded subject, a shared question, a recurring activity,
or a recognizable content proposition.

It is not enough to share:
- A single word.
- The general scope of the channel.
- A tool.
- A brand mention.
- A language.
- Duration or format.
- Publish date.

Sharing those traits does not prevent belonging to the same
group, but is not sufficient justification on its own.

Do not use views to decide group membership.

C. CHECK MEMBERSHIP

Each post must fit directly into the group’s
shared core.

Resembling one post in a group is not enough
if it does not share the group’s core.

If you need to list unrelated subjects to describe
the group, consider splitting it.

A broad name does not fix an incoherent grouping.

Each valid post belongs to a single group or to “Other.”
Do not duplicate posts or metrics.

If a post fits multiple groups:
- Use its main content supported by the text.
- Do not apply preset topic priorities.
- If there is no defensible primary fit, classify it
  as ambiguous within “Other.”

Do not force an assignment just to fill out groups.

D. MINIMUMS AND LIMITS

A topic group needs at least two posts
with a defensible connection.

This is an operational rule for the report, not a scientific
similarity threshold.

There can be:
- Zero groups.
- One group.
- Multiple groups.

Do not invent subdivisions when the entire list forms
one coherent thread.

Do not set a maximum number of posts per group.
All must meet the group’s definition, whether few or many.

Detect defensible groups first without imposing
the document’s space limit.

On the A4 page, show up to five topic groups and,
when applicable, an additional row for “Other.”

If there are more than five groups:
- Show the five with the most posts.
- In case of a tie, prioritize whichever appears first
  in the original order of the list.
- Fold remaining groups into “Other” solely
  to summarize the display.
- Keep their internal assignments.
- Do not present them as unrelated posts.

Order displayed groups from highest to lowest by views.
Place “Other” at the end.

Never merge incoherent groups just to make them fit.

E. HANDLING “OTHER”

“Other” is a presentation category, not a topic
group or proof of dispersion by itself.

Internally keep the reason for each inclusion:
- Standalone post.
- Ambiguous fit.
- Insufficient information.
- Secondary group omitted due to space limit.

Do not send posts to “Other” just for having low views.

Include all of its posts and views in the totals.

Add a brief note under “Other” tailored
to the actual reasons.

If it contains only posts without a clear shared group:
“Posts without a clear shared group.”

If it contains secondary groups:
“Includes secondary groups not broken out separately.”

If it combines reasons:
“Includes ungrouped posts and secondary groups
not broken out separately.”

Do not attribute a shared topic or audience
to “Other” as a whole.

F. IF NO GROUPS ARE FOUND

All posts may end up in “Other.”

State:
“With the available titles and descriptions, similarities are not
clear enough to propose content groups.”

If the main issue is lack of information,
explain that without claiming the content is diverse.

Do not present “Other accounts for 100%” as a finding.
Do not infer that variety hurts the channel.
Do not invent a shared thread, a single audience,
or a specialization recommendation.

G. AUDIT BEFORE CALCULATING

Review all assignments a second time:
- Check each post against its group definition.
- Keep textual evidence.
- Verify that equivalent posts receive
  the same criterion even if language or format differs.
- Pay special attention to boundaries between groups.
- Split, merge, or discard groups if evidence requires it.
- Verify that no post is counted twice.

Do not turn the review into an automatic justification
of the first pass.

Finalize assignments before calculating view distribution.

This check happens internally.
Do not add audit tables or title lists to the A4 page.

11. CALCULATIONS AND INTERNAL VERIFICATION
────────────────────────────────────────

Calculate for the list:
- Valid, unique posts.
- Cumulative views.
- Number of Shorts.
- Number of Videos.
- Number unidentified.
- Views and percentage for each format.

Calculate for each group:
- Posts.
- Cumulative views.
- Share of total views.
- Post count per format.
- Reliance on a single post.
- Available date context.

FORMULAS

Group view share:
group views / views across all valid posts × 100.

Format view share:
format views / views across all valid posts × 100.

Include “Other” and “Unidentified” in the corresponding denominators.

Do not divide by zero.

If total views equal zero:
- Show zero.
- Do not draw fake percentages or breakdowns.
- State that there are no recorded views to calculate a breakdown.

Formatting:
- American English numbers (e.g., commas for thousands, periods for decimals).
- Typically at most one decimal place for percentages.
- No false precision.
- Abbreviated figures from the source remain approximate.

Verify:
- Posts by group = valid total.
- Views by group = total views.
- Posts by format = valid total.
- Views by format = total views.
- No post appears in two groups.
- Percentages use the right denominator.
